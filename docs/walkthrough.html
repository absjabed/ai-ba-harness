The BA handbook

FleetKeep, from setup to ask

One example, start to finish: setting up a project, building its knowledge base, writing an epic, putting it into Jira, changing it afterwards, shipping it, and answering a teammate's question, with a detour into improving the harness itself. Every command appears at least once, with what you type, what it reads, what it asks you and what you end up with. It assumes you have never worked this way. To share it with a team, use the same example as a page of its own: the FleetKeep walkthrough.

An illustration of the design. The harness has not yet run against a real Jira site, so this shows what each command is written to do. Parts 1 to 4 end in real files: the worked example in the template at examples/fleetkeep-ba-knowledge/, which stops on Oct 14. Part 5 goes further than those files. The chat exchanges show what the commands ask; the wording varies from run to run.

The example

FleetKeep, by Northwind Systems, keeps commercial vehicle fleets road-legal: driver walkaround checks, defects, workshop job cards and return to service. Jira project FK on northwind.atlassian.net, Confluence space FK. The pilot customer's workshop keeps missing escalated defects, and the Product owner wants that fixed in release 2026.11. Everything here is fictional.

RoleIn this story
Lead BARuns the commands and maintains the repository. "You" below
BAA second BA on the project, who also applies changes
Product ownerDecides product behaviour, business rules, scope and release content
Engineering leadAnswers technical questions in Jira; decides the technical approach, not rules
Compliance adviserInterprets the regulator's guidance for the product
Pilot transport manager, Pilot workshop supervisorThe customer's people. They give views; they decide nothing in the product
A developerHas a clone of the repository and asks questions with /ask

Words you will see

TermMeans
Knowledge baseThe folder knowledge/: what the project knows, as plain markdown pages, each with an ID, a status and its sources
Source record (SRC-)A short, redacted summary of one input (a meeting, a document, a Jira sync) and what it changed. The raw notes are never committed
Staging fileA proposal in knowledge/_staging/: everything a command wants to add or change, item by item. Nothing in it is knowledge yet
ItemOne proposed change: a new rule, an answer, a term, a risk. You accept, edit or reject each one
VerdictHow an item relates to what is already known: new, confirms, extends, contradicts, duplicate, answers
CriticA second look at every staging file, in a fresh context, before you see it: missed items, wrong quotes, wrong verdicts, data to redact
Statusdraft (unchecked), confirmed (a BA checked it), approved (someone with authority agreed), superseded (replaced; never used in answers)
Capability (CAP-)A page on one thing the product does, with its numbered business rules
Business rule (BR-)One testable rule, such as "a category-B defect escalates after 36 hours"
Decision (DEC-), open question (Q-), action (ACT-)Who decided what; what nobody can answer yet, and who should; who will do what, by when
Requirement documentrequirements/<epic>/requirement.md: the epic's goal, scope, requirements and user stories, written from the knowledge base
Trace maptrace/map.csv: which requirement became which Jira story, and which pages it relies on
ProfilePROJECT-PROFILE.md: the project's settings, filled by /setup

Part 2 · The first knowledge (Sep 17 and 24)

/intake inbox/2026-09-16-discovery-workshop-notes.md

It reads

  • your typed notes from the half-day workshop
  • the knowledge base (empty so far) and stakeholders.md

It asks you

  • to accept, edit or reject each item
  • before pushing, and before deleting the raw notes

You get

  • the first glossary, four capabilities, three entities, a process with its diagram, a decision, two questions, a risk
  • the source record SRC-001

Every command that changes knowledge runs the same five steps:

  1. Extract and judge. Every requirement, rule, decision, question, term and risk in the notes, each with its quote. The Product owner's "the clock starts when the driver reports it" is a decision by someone who can decide. The supervisor and the transport manager gave different accounts of who triages, so that becomes a question (Q-001) instead of a fact.
  2. Stage. One file in knowledge/_staging/, with the exact change for each item.
  3. Critic. A second look in a fresh context: anything left out, quotes that don't match, authority not checked, names to redact. Its findings go at the top of the file.
  4. You decide. "accept 1-24, edit 9: it's the transport manager, not the supervisor, reject 25: that was about another customer".
  5. Apply. Real IDs, pages from templates, the source record, the index and registers rebuilt, a log line, one commit, kb: intake SRC-001 discovery workshop. Then "Push?" and "Delete the raw notes from inbox/?" (default: keep).

What a page looks like afterwards (from CAP-004, as it stands in the example):

## Rules applied

1. **BR-017** The escalation clock starts when the defect is reported. (DEC-001)
2. **BR-015** A category-A defect still in Reported or Triaged 4 hours after it was
   reported is escalated to the transport manager. (SRC-001, FK-1188)
3. **BR-016** A category-B defect still in Reported or Triaged 36 hours after it was
   reported is escalated to the transport manager. (FK-1188)

A week later the Compliance adviser sends a summary of the regulator's guidance as a PDF:

/intake inbox/regulator-roadworthiness-guidance.pdf

Same steps. PDFs are read directly; a Word file would go through markitdown first. The covering note asks what happens when a driver has no signal at the depot. Nobody can answer that yet, so it becomes Q-003, addressed to the Product owner. This time you say yes to deleting the PDF from inbox/; the source record SRC-002 keeps the summary.

Part 3 · Every day (Oct 6 to 16)

/sync jira

It reads

  • FK issues in the scope JQL changed since the last sync, with their comments and history
  • the source records, so nothing is proposed twice

It asks you

  • first run only: how far back to go ("since Sep 28")
  • to decide each staged item

You get

  • Q-001 answered from a Jira comment
  • CAP-003 superseded by CAP-004 (category B escalates too)
  • DEC-002 (confirmed) and DEC-003 (approved); source record SRC-003

It reads Jira and never writes to it, not even a comment, and ignores routine status moves, estimates and assignee changes. Watch the authority check: the Engineering lead's comment says the Product owner agreed something at stand-up. That is second-hand, so DEC-002 stays confirmed, not approved, until the Product owner confirms it. The sync time moves only after you have decided every item. After the first run it's a daily habit: two to ten items most mornings.
